## Contents
Below is a small sample of the early process of building the foundation of the rubric refining methodology that I'm still actively iterating on. They aren't full polished documents, but they should be legible with a little patience. For those not comfortable with .md or ai generated files, the order of most legible to least legible is:

- **starting-domain-expertise-from-operator.md** -- this is the extraction of my own 10 years of writing linkedin posts (😀🔫). Any rubric will need some quality floor to begin with. Mechanical things like character counts before wrapping, you need unicode to bold text on LinkedIn, that kind of stuff. This first iteration blended too much of the mechanical with my individual taste. i've gotten better at finding that line since.
- **refinement-vector-2-full-rewrites.md** -- this is the grunt work part of it. It's taking agentic output, and not just redlining it, but rewriting it entirely with extensive notes. For client work, this is going to be the major bottleneck. They aren't going to do this. But most corporate brands are 90% the same, so it's just a matter of tweaking word choice, expecially in non-native-english countries in the tech industry: how much of modern tech words stay English, even in their native language. That's the biggest dial to tweak for CEE region. And that's very doable.
- **refinement-vector-1-critiques.md** -- originally meant to be a quick middle ground of dropbox grading and re-writes. First iteration was basically just partial re-writes. Now it's more like a comment-on-a-google-doc redlining experience.
- **alderman-ai-linkedin-grading-rubric.md** -- an early version, which had 17 categories of grading, with only 3 possible grades: (unpostable | good | great). I've since added postable as the second of four. Most clients are perfectly fine with 'postable' and it requires very little to acheive -- basically the domain expertise extraction plus some minor refinements. The 'good' as a floor is mostly for marketing agencies.
