## Hook formatting
There are two, primary hook formats for my style. For now, not following one of these is a hard fail. There are exceptions I make to these, but we will handle edge cases once the core rubric is stronger.

### Double hook | Sandwich hook | Default hook | Hook style 1
Default hook style. I've written ~2000 LinkedIn posts, and roughly ~75% are in this style.

**Hook specs**
Two lines of text, each ≤85 chars including emojis, separated by an empty line of white space. The first line will appear above the fold on mobile, and both lines will appear above the fold on desktop.

**Example**
```
🤔 You know who knows how to structure excellent prompts?  
  
The engineers building flagship ai platforms. 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗺! 🔽
```

**Example 2**
```
🎨 Creating custom coloring book images with you kids is really fun.  
  
Here's some sample posts and their results if you want to try it. 👇
```

### Paragraph hook | Secondary hook | Hook style 2
Secondary hook style. As a human, I use this style when I find really good copy for a hook, but can't trim it down to fit hook 1's specs. So instead I expand the middle between the two parts of the hook I was working on. 

**Hook specs**
One paragraph of text that is inclusively between 100–240 characters including emojis. The entire paragraph should display above the fold on Desktop, and nothing below the first paragraph should be visible above the fold.


**Example**
```
🔎 Agentic workflows 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗤𝗔 𝗹𝗼𝗼𝗽𝘀 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗻𝗼 𝗹𝗼𝗻𝗴𝗲𝗿 "𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲". In 2026 they're non-negotiable. But not all QA is created equal. Here's how to go from intermediate to advanced 👇
```

**Example 2**
```
🔥🚗The road to hell is paved with good intentions… and 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗼𝗮𝗱 𝘁𝗼 𝗮 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗹𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘂𝗻𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗱 𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄𝗹𝗲𝗱𝗴𝗲 𝗯𝗮𝘀𝗲 𝗶𝘀 𝟳𝟬% 𝗮𝗰𝗰𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘆. Here's why a partially accurate KB is even worse than no KB 👇
```

## General Lexicon

A non exhaustive list of my personal post-writing shorthand. Some of these may be used by others from my domain, but most are my own personal lexicon. 

### Bold

- Important! If I ever say "bold" in a LinkedIn context, I mean using unicode bold (sans) symbols instead of the plain-text letter equivalents within the string I tell you to "bold." **Example:** 
	- Make "The rain in Spain falls mostly on the plains" bold should result in: 
		- 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗦𝗽𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝗳𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘀 𝗺𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗹𝘆 𝗼𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻𝘀
	- **Bad example / Hard fail:**
		- `**The rain in Spain falls mostly on the plains**`
		- because LinkedIn doesn't render markdown syntax
### Emphasis | Emphasis markers

Anything that adds emphasis to a post. This can be to catch the eye, or to apply a designer's sense of balance within the visual elements of the copy.

- **Common categories (in descending order of frequency):**
	- Single emojis
	- 𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗲𝘅𝘁
	- Expressive punctuation ( ... | ! | !! | ?? )
	- CAPS
	- 👉 finger sandwiches 👈
	

### Above the fold | Above the line | ATF | ATL
Any copy that is shown above the "read more" button. Desktop typically gets three rendered lines. Mobile ~2. 

### Invite the click | Click-TA
- The part of the hook that actually drives someone to click. This is not tautological. Hooks are both set-up, and then delivery. 
- Clicks should be explicitly invited
	- **Important:** You can write a SUPER CLEAN, and SUPER PUNCHY ... just BEAUTIFUL "hook". But if it doesn't invite the click, it's not actually a hook. It's just a 3 line post to 99.5% of people who didn't click 'read more' because they didn't see any reason to.
- The Click-TA is the final copy above the fold (i.e. right before the breakpoint) that invites the click, **Examples:**
	- Find out how 👇


### Break point
Where the above the fold copy ends. The last of the copy that appear before truncated.

- The break point is the last fully visible and non-obscured character above the fold
- Break points should be deliberate, not accidental.
- Break points should not be in the middle of a sentence or idea
	- UNLESS PURPOSEFULLY DONE SO TO PROVOKE CURIOSITY
		-  Weight this extremely low. It's usually the wrong choice.
- Break points should invite the click

## Emoji Lexicon
These categories can bleed into each other at edge cases. The most important thing in this section is that if I say "Bad Descriptive Emoji" it means that the emoji they 'strategically' paired with the text is a bad fit. **Due to either:**
- **Semantic issues:** e.g.: I like frogs 🐰; I just read Art of the Deal 🚂
- **Rendering issues:** i.e. some emojis just look bad or ambiguous, even if they semantically are a good fit. e.g.: 🏦Banking is interesting; News about the health industry 🏥 ---  readers can't easily clear what those images are, so it looks random

### Descriptive | Semantic emoji
An emoji that connects to the nearby text. 
**Examples:**
- our numbers went up 📈
- ⏩ we made something go faster
- this makes no sense 🙃
- 1️⃣ point number one (note: I may be inconsistent in calling this descriptive/non-descriptive. it's borderline)

### Semantic Bullet emojis

 Some semantic emojis can be used as bullet emojis IFF the emoji fits the semantic meaning of the line of text in the list item.

- **Categories:**
	- **Binary:** ✅ | ❌ ; 👍 | 👎
	- **Freeform:** anything. Basically it's a cluster of three descriptive emojis semantically paired with short lines of text, stacked on top of each other. The emoji must be left of text and is allowed to violate emoji balance (left | right) rules.
 
- **Cluster Size:** 
	- Semantic bullet typically come in clusters of 3-4. Most often 3. 5 is too many.
	- A single line semantic emoji connected to a line of text isn't a bullet emoji, just a normal semantic emoji
	- Pairs of two are possible, but often used only as contrasting examples.
		- A two-item like-like list of bullets should just be written as a normal paragraph.


**COMMON BINARY CLUSTERS**
```
**Example 1**:
✅ <something that is correct or good 1>
✅ <something that is correct or good 2>
✅ <something that is correct or good 3>

**Example 2**:
👎 <something that is incorrect or bad 1>
👎 <something that is incorrect or bad 2>
👎 <something that is incorrect or bad 3>

**Example 3**
✅ <something that is correct or good 1>
✅ <something that is correct or good 2>
❌ <something that is incorrect or bad>
/// ^ emphasis on the bad|incorrect thing

**Example 4**:
👎 <something that is incorrect or bad 1>
👎 <something that is incorrect or bad 2>
👍 <something that is correct or good>
/// ^ emphasis on the good|correct thing
```

**Note:** It's almost always better to use ✅|❌ instead of 👍|👎because the former pair contrasts both in direction AND color; making the distinction instant. NEVER MIX THESE TWO PAIRS

**CONTRASTING PAIR EXAMPLE**
```
Want to not reinvent the wheel while building in ai?
❌ Build everything from scratch
✅ Vendor skills from public repos!
```


**FREEFORM SEMANTIC BULLET CLUSTER**
```
Three reasons to like the Czech Republic:
🏰 Castles
🚋 Public Transportation
🍺 Great beer!
```
